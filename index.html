Gene Regulatory Network Inference in the Presence of Selection Bias and Latent Confounders

NeurIPS 2025 · View paper
1. Assumptions

Prior knowledge and causal interpretation

Based on prior knowledge, we consider three mechanisms that shape gene expression: regulatory interactions between genes, unobserved factors that regulate multiple genes, and constraints induced by selection. Although biologically distinct, all three can manifest statistically as conditional dependencies among genes. In causal terminology, we refer to them as regulation, latent confounding, and selection, respectively.

Regulation

Gene regulatory networks encode causal interactions through transcriptional regulation and downstream molecular processes. The activity of one gene can therefore alter another gene's expression and induce a statistical dependency.

X → Y

Latent confounder

Two genes may share an unobserved cause, such as a non-coding RNA or an environmental stimulus. This common influence can make their expression levels dependent even when neither gene directly regulates the other.

X ← L → Y

Selection

Evolutionary and cellular viability constraints determine which expression profiles can persist. Only cells satisfying survival or inclusion criteria are observed and sequenced, so conditioning on the selected population can induce a dependency between otherwise independent genes. Such constraints may reflect competition for shared cellular resources or differential activity across promoters and enhancers.

X → S ← Y   (observe S = 1)

These mechanisms are not mutually exclusive: regulation, latent confounding, and selection may coexist for the same gene pair.
